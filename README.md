# Income Inequality in New York City

## Project Overview

This Tableau project explores income inequality across New York City's five boroughs and neighborhoods.

Using **American Community Survey (ACS) 2018–2022 5-Year Estimates** together with New York City's official Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs), the dashboard examines how income, poverty, housing costs, racial concentration, household composition, education, employment, gender earnings, and disability vary across the city.

The project is guided by the following question:

> **Where and why is economic disparity most pronounced in New York City, and how do these contrasts define the city's economic landscape?**

---

## Dashboard Sections

### 1. Spatial Analysis of Income Inequality

This section examines geographic differences in:

* Median household income
* Poverty rates
* Rent burden
* Borough-level economic conditions
* Neighborhood-level income patterns

The dashboard highlights substantial differences between boroughs. Manhattan and Staten Island report the highest median household incomes, while the Bronx experiences the lowest income, highest poverty rate, and highest rent burden.

Neighborhood-level maps reveal sharp income divides throughout Manhattan and western Brooklyn.

---

### 2. The Racial Income Divide

This section explores the relationship between neighborhood racial concentration and economic outcomes.

Neighborhoods are classified according to their dominant racial or ethnic population as either:

* Majority (>50%)
* Plurality (40–50%)
* Mixed

The analysis compares:

* Median household income
* Poverty rates
* Rent burden
* Borough distribution

The dashboard demonstrates that neighborhood racial concentration is strongly associated with economic outcomes. Majority White neighborhoods have substantially higher median incomes, while Majority Hispanic neighborhoods experience lower incomes together with higher poverty and rent burden.

---

### 3. Household Composition and Income

This section investigates how household composition changes across boroughs and neighborhood income groups.

The analysis compares:

* Median household income
* Family household concentration
* Borough-level household structure
* Household composition across income categories

One notable finding is the contrast between Staten Island and Manhattan. Although both have relatively high median household incomes, Staten Island contains a much larger share of family households.

The dashboard also identifies an **Affluent Anomaly**, where neighborhoods earning more than $120,000 annually contain significantly fewer family households than many middle-income neighborhoods.

---

### 4. Structural Drivers of Inequality

The final section explores four socioeconomic factors associated with neighborhood income:

* Educational attainment
* Employment status
* Gender earnings
* Disability status

Neighborhoods are grouped into four income categories:

* Below $40,000
* $40,000–$80,000
* $80,000–$120,000
* Above $120,000

The analysis finds strong relationships between educational attainment, employment, and neighborhood wealth.

Higher-income neighborhoods contain substantially larger shares of residents with advanced degrees and higher employment rates. The gender earnings gap also widens considerably in wealthier neighborhoods, while disability status remains relatively consistent across income groups.

---

# Key Findings

* Large geographic differences in household income exist across New York City's boroughs.
* Lower-income neighborhoods experience substantially higher poverty and rent burden.
* Neighborhood racial concentration is strongly associated with economic outcomes.
* Educational attainment and employment are among the strongest predictors of neighborhood wealth.
* The gender earnings gap increases alongside neighborhood income.
* Household composition differs significantly between affluent urban and suburban communities.

# Data Sources

### American Community Survey (ACS) 2018–2022 5-Year Estimates — U.S. Census Bureau

Provides detailed demographic, social, economic, and housing data at multiple geographic levels, including Census Tracts. These data were used to calculate measures such as median household income, poverty, rent burden, educational attainment, employment status, disability, household composition, and demographic characteristics.

https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/acs-5year.html

---

### 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs) Shapefile — NYC Open Data

Provides the official geographic boundaries for New York City's Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs), which were used to perform neighborhood-level spatial analysis and create the dashboard maps.

https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/2020-Neighborhood-Tabulation-Areas-NTAs-/9nt8-h7nd

---

### NYC Department of City Planning — American Community Survey (ACS)

Provides ACS datasets together with Neighborhood Tabulation Area (NTA) geographies and geographic crosswalks used to connect Census Tract-level ACS data with NYC neighborhoods.

https://www.nyc.gov/content/planning/pages/resources/datasets/american-community-survey
